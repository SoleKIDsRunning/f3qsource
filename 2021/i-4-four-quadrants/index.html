I.4

Four Quadrants

The Q is a leader who takes responsibility for the Outcome17 in the Groups of which he is a Member and the QSource is the Wisdom a leader needs to be Effective. It is divided into Four Quadrants:

  • Q1 is The DRP (the Daily Red Pill)—the Q’s daily commitment to accelerate his fitness, fellowship and faith in order to be a more Effective leader. Fitness is a man’s relationship with himself, fellowship is his relationship with other people and faith is his relationship with that which cannot be seen but is believed to exist.
  • Q2 is IMPACT—the forcible contact to strong effect that the Q has on the other Members of his Groups.
  • Q3 is Group Leadership—the leadership skills and characteristics the Q employs to IMPACT, build and develop.
  • Q4 is Legacy, what the Q leaves behind as a results of his IMPACT and leadership.

Each of the Four Quadrants is comprised of a series of QPoints that are designed to sequentially build upon one another. Each QPoint has five components that begin with the letter “S”. The Five-S’s are designed to convey the essence of the QPoint and make it both memorable and easy to pass along within the leadership development process.

  • Statement: the organizing principle of the QPoint. The Statement provides the QPoint’s definition and reason for its inclusion in the Quadrant.
  • Scripture: a passage to exemplify the QPoint and demonstrate its timeless nature.
  • Socratic: a series of questions designed to elicit thought and discussion about the QPoint.
  • Synthesis: a short essay expounding and explaining the QPoint. Sometimes it will tell a story. Sometimes it will be more thematic. The Synthesis broadens the principle provided by the Statement and provides practical examples of its application.
  • Spur: the concise takeaway from the QPoint, like points on a compass that help the Q find his way under stress and faced with a leadership dilemma.
