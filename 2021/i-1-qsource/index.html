I.1

Qsource

Seriously, another book about leadership?

If that is your first reaction, it would be a fair one. I’ve read at least a thousand leadership books and every time I download a new one I ask myself that same question, another leadership book, seriously? But I keep going and read it anyway, and the reason I do that is simple: my leadership is not as effective as I want and need it to be.

When it comes to leadership books, we could divide the world into three populations.

  • POPULATION ONE: doesn’t think about leadership much; doesn’t care whether they are effective leaders; doesn’t care whether they are led by effective leaders—doesn’t read leadership books
  • POPULATION TWO: thinks about leadership anywhere from a little to a lot; believes that they could exert more effective leadership; wants to be led by effective leaders—reads leadership books (anywhere from a few to dozens)
  • POPULATION THREE: thinks ineffective leadership lies at the heart of all the problems of the world; won’t stop seeking increased leadership effectiveness until they die; demands effective leadership from others (and is willing to help them improve)—writes leadership books.

So, if you are a Population One person, don’t bother with this book. In fact, that is a bit of an empty statement since you are unlikely to have gotten this far. If you are Population Three, I won’t bother trying to persuade you to read further because (like me) you are driven by your own star anyway. But if you are a Population One PAX1, this book should be one of the few or dozen you read because it is aimed at your twin desires to be a more effective leader and to be led by more effective leaders.

If you are still reading, we’ll go ahead now and unpack something that might bug you a little bit about this book (and our leadership philosophy in general). As the first footnote indicated, we have our own definitions for many words that already appear to have perfectly serviceable definitions. That might sound a bit Orwellian, but (unlike 1984) the purpose is not to limit freedom of thought but to unlimber it by decalcifying the language of leadership and make it useful for F3’s purpose, which is to reinvigorate male community leadership.

In explanation, take accountability as an example. If you look that word up in the dictionary you will find some form of the following definition: an obligation or willingness to

accept responsibility or to account for one's actions. That definition, while linguistically accurate, isn’t very helpful because it doesn’t tell you how you do it.

The culture has further maligned the word into full-blown uselessness. Smart people (i.e., those who should know better) routinely say things like “yup, this is the year I start holding myself accountable!” That sounds good, but it’s a useless statement because nobody can hold themselves accountable. If that were possible, the word accountability would not need to exist because the concept of accountability would be unnecessary—we would just all do the right thing. And we don’t, at least not enough of time to render the world free of the problems that the concept of accountability arose to address in the first place.

As a result, for the purpose of the language of leadership, the word “accountability” has been denatured by the cultured and rendered useless. Which is not good, because the concept of “accountability” is inexpressibly important to the skill of leadership.

So we redefined it. Within F3, Accountability (note the capital “A”) is submission to standard through enforcement and consequence. To practice Accountability there must be a Standard (an objective measure of performance or behavior), Enforcement (an external force to apply the Standard) and Consequence (the result of the Enforcement of the Standard).

When we say Accountability, that is what we mean. Just as when we say PAX, we mean people. These are just two examples of the F3 Lexicon, which is our particularized language of leadership. We are not alone in this. Every Effective2 group has its own language of leadership, whether it be the United States Army or the New England Patriots. The Army has field manuals that are full of its unique doctrine and lexicon and the Patriots have their playbook, which performs the same essential function. Both a military field manual and a football playbook provide the members of those two groups with that which provides the group with Advantage3.

Like the Army and the Patriots, F3 is also an organization that seeks Advantage. It is comprised of a network of men’s small workout groups. It began Charlotte that began in Charlotte with a single group of men. Initially the group had no other purpose than to provide men with a free opportunity to workout and spend time together. But it quickly developed into something more than that. Because each group is self-led on a rotating basis by its members, we found that F3 also provides a leadership opportunity—something we could see that men needed as much as they did the physical exercise and sense of community that the group provided.

Recognizing that need, we wrote Freed To Lead: Unleashing The Modern Day Warrior to establish F3’s mission and explain how and why it works. We intended Freed To Lead to be a

guidebook for the men who were stepping up as leaders within their groups. We came to refer to these men by the letter “Q”, which means the leader, the man who takes responsibility for what happens and fails to happen. The Advantage that F3 has obtained has come wholly from the efforts of its Qs to lead and their willingness to take responsibility for the outcome.

The Q is a virtuous man who leads Effectively for the benefit of others. He has both the skill and character required to be what a leader is and to do what a leader does. The QSource is the collective wisdom from which the Q may draw to develop his leadership skills as well as those of other leaders in his community and the groups of which he is a member.

The QSource comes from our education and experiences as leaders and (more importantly) from what we have learned from the men who have helped us grow F3. As we travel around the various regions that comprise F3Nation, we adhere to a simple process called ALR4. Whenever we have the opportunity to watch other leaders in action, we always ask questions, listen to the answer and remember what we heard. Then, after we have talked about what we heard a lot and tried it out for ourselves, we write it down so other men can test it. What we have gathered here in the QSource are things we have learned that have passed the test.

As the organization grew, F3’s Mission (the what it does + the why it does it) evolved into to what it is today: to plant, serve and grow men’s small workout groups in order to reinvigorate male community leadership. We have learned a tremendous amount about leadership in the last six years in our efforts to perform the Mission, both from what has worked and what has failed. It has formed a body of truth that has been hard-earned and hard- learned, what we call Wisdom. It is to distinguish this Wisdom from say, the Wisdom of Bill Belichick, that we have called it the QSource.

As with many of concepts in our leadership philosophy, we borrowed the name from another organization. The “Q Source” is the hypothetical body of the common material that formed the basis of the synoptic gospels in the New Testament. Nobody has ever found the “Q Source Document”, but it is believed to have evolved from the oral tradition that formed the basis of the Christian movement.

We didn’t choose this name to give our leadership philosophy a theological stamp. F3 is not a religious group, it is a leadership organization that draws its strength from any source that is Effective. Simply put, the idea of an evolving body of Wisdom that provides the foundation for a useful doctrine (which is what the Q Source is) dovetails neatly into our preexisting use of the letter Q to identify our leaders. Within F3, “Q” is the leader and the Source of what he knows to be Effective is Q + Source = QSource. The fact that the word has been used previously

by another successful movement5 also appeals to us. F3 is a movement which (like every movement before it), needs a doctrine.

The QSource is that doctrine.
